## The problem

LIXI publishes the data standards that Australian banks, brokers and lenders use to exchange loan information. Its lending glossary of 811 terms had been curated by hand over time, and had duplicate terms and missing definitions.

## How it works

I built a retrieval-augmented pipeline that drafts a definition for each term, grounded in 15 authoritative sources including the ATO, ASIC, APRA and the RBA.

<Note text="Running on-device was a data privacy decision: nothing is sent to an outside API.">

Everything runs on one machine. Sources are embedded locally with sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), the top five passages for each term are found with cosine similarity over NumPy arrays, and Llama 3 8B, running through Ollama, writes the draft. The whole thing is packaged as a Tkinter desktop app.

</Note>

## Key decisions

**Every definition traces back to a source.** Prompt guardrails enforce source grounding and citation constraints, and the output is validated, so a reviewer can check each definition against where it came from.

<Note text="A flagged term needs a human decision about which sense the glossary means, so these went to review rather than being resolved by the model.">

**Find the ambiguous terms automatically.** Some lending terms mean different things in different contexts. Clustering each term's definition embeddings, with average-linkage agglomerative clustering on a cosine-distance threshold, flagged 61 terms that likely have more than one sense.

</Note>

**Automated checks first, then a person.** A script checked the first 100 definitions for length, refusal and boilerplate phrases, how closely each definition matched its term, and how much it duplicated the retrieved source text. A manual spot check then caught what the automated checks couldn't: factual errors, outdated references and wrong issuers. I corrected these and handed the output over as drafts for expert review.
